MODEL RELIABILITY AND AUC RULES
=========================================================

For questions such as:

- How reliable is the habitat suitability model?
- What does the AUC value indicate?
- Can the predictions be considered reliable?
- Is the model performance high, moderate, or weak?

Use:

- Training AUC
- #Training samples
- #Background points

AUC means Area Under the Curve.

Explain it in user-friendly terms:

AUC measures how well the model distinguishes between more suitable and less suitable environmental conditions. Higher AUC values indicate better discrimination. An AUC close to 0.5 would indicate little discrimination, while values closer to 1 indicate stronger discrimination.

Interpret Training AUC as follows:

- AUC below 0.6: weak discrimination
- AUC 0.6 to below 0.7: limited discrimination
- AUC 0.7 to below 0.8: acceptable discrimination
- AUC 0.8 to below 0.9: good discrimination
- AUC 0.9 or above: very strong discrimination

If the only AUC value is Training AUC, say that it reflects model discrimination on the training data.

Do not claim independent validation unless an independent test AUC, cross-validation result, or external validation metric is present in KNOWLEDGE.

For reliability, combine performance and caution:

- If Training AUC is 0.8 or above but below 0.9, say the model shows good discrimination on the training data.
- If Training AUC is 0.9 or above, say the model shows very strong discrimination on the training data.
- If no test AUC or independent validation metric is provided, say the model should be interpreted cautiously because independent validation is not reported.
- If #Training samples is present and below 50, mention that the limited number of training samples adds uncertainty.
- If #Background points is present, you may mention it as part of the modelling setup.
- Do not say the model is fully reliable, proven, or certain.

For AUC questions, answer with:
1. what AUC means;
2. the exact Training AUC value;
3. the performance class;
4. the reliability caveat if independent validation is not reported.

Example answer pattern for AUC:

"AUC, or Area Under the Curve, measures how well the model distinguishes between more suitable and less suitable environmental conditions. For [species], the Training AUC is [Training AUC], which indicates [AUC class] on the training data. This supports useful model discrimination, but because no independent test AUC or cross-validation metric is provided, reliability should be interpreted cautiously."

Example answer pattern for reliability:

"The habitat suitability model for [species] has a Training AUC of [Training AUC], indicating [AUC class] on the training data. This suggests the model can distinguish suitable from less suitable conditions reasonably well. However, because the provided output does not include independent validation, the predictions should be considered useful but not fully confirmed."
